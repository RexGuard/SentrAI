The problem

Small organizations get the alert. Nobody acts in time.

Singapore SMEs and private schools hold large amounts of personal data with one or two IT staff and no security team. The breach is rarely a surprise: the warning was there, it was just never acted on.

How it works

From log line to accountable decision, in four steps.

A collector reads what your systems already log, AI classifies it, the risk engine scores it, and the responder acts only inside your own network, always reversibly.

  1. Collect

    Web, database and OS logs are gathered per layer. Scout helps a novice technician find where logs live on unfamiliar systems.

  2. Classify

    Rules plus AI categorize each event (brute force, SQL injection, exfiltration, misconfiguration) with a confidence that feeds the score.

  3. Score

    A live 0 to 100 risk index with an inaction penalty, so an ignored alert gets louder every hour instead of fading away.

  4. Respond

    Past your threshold, CactAI blocks or locks with a time limit, escalates up the ladder, and writes every step to a hash-chained log.

What makes it different

Built for teams of one or two, not a 24/7 SOC.

Every automatic action is temporary, every decision is recorded, and a human always has the final say.

Risk grows with inaction

Unaddressed alerts add points every hour past the SLA, so neglect is visible on the gauge long before it becomes a breach.

Every fix expires

Automatic blocks carry a time limit (default 2 hours). A person must make them permanent, extend them, or roll them back.

Tamper-evident evidence

A hash-chained audit log and evidence report show what was detected, who was told, and what was done, for PDPA Protection Obligation reviews.

Cyanide, the AI planner

An AI incident planner picks containment steps for your system profile, while scoring, time limits and the audit trail stay deterministic.

Ask before you approve

Operators chat with Cyanide to understand an incident. Suggestions arrive as buttons that go through the normal approval gates.

Escalation that reaches people

Dashboard and Telegram alerts climb from the on-duty operator to the team lead, IT manager and CXO as the risk band rises.

The cactus principle

Left alone, it does no harm. Attack it, and it pricks you.

CactAI never hacks back. Striking an attacker's machine is an offence under Singapore's Computer Misuse Act, and attacks usually come through hijacked computers owned by other victims. The spines stay on the cactus.

What CactAI does, inside your network

  • Blocks IPs, locks accounts and rate-limits logins
  • Slows attackers down with tarpits
  • Plants honeytokens that raise a near-certain alert when touched
  • Preserves evidence for SingCERT or the police

What CactAI never does

  • Access, scan or disrupt the attacker's systems
  • Make a fix permanent without a human decision
  • Change anything outside a pre-approved playbook
  • Claim an alert was read when it was only delivered

Working prototype

A fictional student portal gets attacked. CactAI handles it.

The MVP runs on one Windows laptop: attack scripts hit the demo portal, CactAI scores the risk, alerts the operator, blocks the attackers when the score crosses 80, and writes a hash-chained evidence report. One script starts everything, and automated tests cover the full story end to end.
